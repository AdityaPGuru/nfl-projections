3. Late-season width diagnostic

The calculations are correct for the stated pooled diagnostic:

- Prediction width is the sample standard deviation within each week bucket.
- Calibration slope is OLS slope of actual on projected, implemented as `np.polyfit(projected, actual, 1)[0]`.
- A slope above 1 correctly means predictions are too narrow.

The result supports the claim:

- Naive SD declines from `0.0501` in Weeks 1–4 to `0.0463` in Weeks 13–18.

Caveat: this is pooled across seasons and games, not a within-season or team-balanced decomposition. It accurately describes the prediction population but does not by itself prove that every individual team’s uncertainty evolves that way.

4. Relative-to-league target and drift

No leakage or erroneous double-counting found.

- `y_rel = y - lg_prev` and all share predictors are centered on the same prior-season league level.
- Final predictions add `lg_prev` back exactly once.
- `lg_gap` represents prior-season league level minus the mean of all league seasons before S, including S−1 in that historical mean. That is a legitimate reversion feature, though the inclusive reference mean slightly mechanically dampens the gap.
- `lg_drift` is current-season league share through W−1 minus `lg_prev`.
- Both can appear alongside centered team shares without algebraic duplication: `lg_gap` describes between-season regime/reversion, while `lg_drift` describes observed within-season movement.
