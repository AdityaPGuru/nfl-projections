2. The claimed 0.32–0.35 “ceiling” is not a defensible hard ceiling. [README.md:9](/Users/AdityaGuruHome/Documents/screenshots/Coding/Projects/nfl-projections/player_state/README.md:9)–[README.md:12](/Users/AdityaGuruHome/Documents/screenshots/Coding/Projects/nfl-projections/player_state/README.md:12) treats marginal per-attempt dispersion divided by approximately √33 as wholly unpredictable independent luck. That requires independent, identically distributed play errors and assumes none of the within-game dispersion is predictable from pregame information. Neither is established.

   Likewise, converting a random split-half correlation to √reliability gives a reliability-based estimate only under parallel halves, independent errors, and a stable latent game mean. Football plays have shared game state, sequencing, possession, score, and selection effects. A random partition of one realized game also does not demonstrate an information-theoretic bound on every pregame predictor.

   Therefore 0.32–0.35 is a useful noise/reliability diagnostic, not proof that “nothing honest can” exceed it. No script or `out/*.csv` reproduces the stated 1.87, 1.77, 0.59, or 0.067 inputs either.

   Impact: changes the broad conclusion that the leaked 0.511/0.544 controls are impossible solely because they exceed 0.35. Their large values remain highly suspicious, and the clean H1 result strongly contradicts them, but the proposed ceiling does not prove leakage by itself.
